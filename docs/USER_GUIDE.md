# User guide

For everyone at T&C. No technical background needed — if you can use email, you
can use this.

Part 2 is for managers.

---

# Part 1 — For employees

## What this is

T&C AI Academy works out how much you already know about using AI at work, then
builds you a personal learning plan of about 35 hours — courses chosen for your
job, your level and the time you actually have.

**It is not a test of you.** The first assessment has no pass mark. Its only
purpose is to start you in the right place. Answering honestly gets you a useful
plan; guessing gets you a plan that wastes your time.

## Getting started

**Sign in** with your employee ID or work email and the password you were given.
You will be asked to change it the first time.

**Tell us about your work** — 2 minutes. Your department, your job, the tasks
you do most days, what you would like AI to help with, and how much time you can
give to learning each week. Be realistic about the time: two honest hours a week
produces a better plan than five optimistic ones.

**Take the placement assessment** — 20 short questions, 25 minutes, in your own
language.

- Practical questions about using AI at work. No programming.
- No preparation needed. No trick questions.
- Your answers save as you go — you can close it and come back.
- There is a timer, but nobody is watching you.
- Answer honestly. Guessing high lands you in courses that are too hard.

**Read your result.** You get a level from L0 to L4, five bars showing where you
are strong and where you are not, and your recommended learning path — with the
reason each course was chosen.

| Level | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **L0** Beginner | You have not used AI at work yet. Perfectly normal, and the right place to start |
| **L1** Aware | You have tried AI but do not have a reliable method |
| **L2** Practitioner | You use AI regularly for real work |
| **L3** Advanced | You design prompts and workflows others can use |
| **L4** Expert | You build and evaluate AI solutions (requires the technical assessment) |

Nobody is ranked against anybody else, and your individual answers are not shown
to your manager.

## Your learning path

About 35 hours, in phases, in the order that works:

**Understand AI → Practise with AI → Apply it to your job → Data and automation
→ Use it responsibly**

You do not have to choose. Open **My Learning** and the biggest button on the
page is the next thing to do. If you only ever press that button, you will
finish the whole path in the right order.

Some courses are on external sites — Google, Microsoft and others. Follow the
link, do the course there, then come back and upload your completion
certificate. Everything else runs inside the Academy: read a lesson, answer a
short quiz, move on.

**Your progress saves automatically**, page by page. Stop whenever you like.

## Along the way

- **Module quizzes** — short, retakeable. They exist to make things stick, not
  to catch you out.
- **The prompt library** — ready-made prompts for real tasks. Copy, adapt, save
  your own.
- **AI use cases** — what colleagues in your department actually use AI for.
- **My AI Toolbox** — the tools approved for use at T&C, and what each is for.
- **The AI coach** — a small button on lesson pages. Ask it anything about what
  you are reading. It only knows your own lesson and your own progress.

## Finishing

**Final assessment** — 30 questions, 40 minutes, scored against the same five
areas as your first one, so you see exactly how far you have come.

**Responsible AI** — a short mandatory module and assessment. Required before
any certificate. It covers what you may and may not put into an AI tool at work.
Please take it seriously; this is the part with real consequences.

**Your capstone** — one real improvement in your own job, using AI. Not an
essay. Describe the task, what you did, what changed, and how long it now takes.
Your manager reviews it. Good ones become AI opportunities the company acts on.

**Your certificate** — issued when the path is complete, the final assessment is
passed, the Responsible AI module is passed and the capstone is approved. It is
a PDF with a QR code; anyone can scan it to confirm it is genuine. Your
certificates page shows exactly which of the four requirements are still
outstanding, so there is never a mystery.

**Your AI Skill Passport** — your before-and-after: level, hours, competencies,
certificates and capstones, in one page you can show anyone.

Then you get a new recommendation for the next level.

## Practical answers

**In Arabic or Turkish?** Yes. Change it in Profile → Language, or from the
switcher at the top. Arabic runs fully right-to-left.

**On my phone?** Yes, everything.

**Can I stop halfway through the assessment?** Yes. Answers save as you go and
it reopens where you left it.

**Can I retake it?** The placement assessment allows two attempts, with a
waiting period. Later assessments allow more. Module quizzes are unlimited.

**I forgot my password.** Use *Forgot password* on the sign-in page. If no email
arrives, contact your Learning & Development team — email may not be switched on
in your installation.

**How much time do I need?** Whatever you told us — the plan is built around it.
Two hours a week finishes a 35-hour path in about four months.

**Does my manager see my answers?** No. They see your level, your progress and
your capstone. Not your individual assessment answers, not your coach
conversations.

**A course link is broken.** Report it from the course page. Broken links are
removed from everyone's recommendations.

**I disagree with my level.** It is a starting point, not a verdict. Talk to your
Learning & Development team — a retake can be arranged.

# Part 2 — For managers

You keep everything above, plus a **Team** section.

## What you can see

For your direct reports: current level, learning path progress, completed
courses, certificates, capstone submissions and anything overdue.

## What you cannot see

Individual assessment answers. AI coach conversations. Anyone outside your team.
This is a development tool, not a monitoring tool — and it is built that way on
purpose, because people who feel monitored answer assessments dishonestly, and
then the whole thing is worthless.

## What to do with it

**Nominate learning.** If someone needs a specific course, nominate it. It
appears at the top of their path, labelled as coming from you, so they know why.

**Review capstones.** This is the part that matters most. A capstone is one real
improvement someone made to their own job. Approve it, or send it back with a
specific comment about what would make it stronger. A vague "needs more detail"
helps nobody.

**Escalate the good ones.** A strong capstone is a process improvement your
department can adopt. Flagging it as an AI opportunity puts it in front of
leadership.

## Using it well

- **Talk about it as development.** The moment it is treated as an appraisal
  input, results become fiction.
- **Protect the time.** Two hours a week that nobody protects is zero hours a
  week.
- **Watch for stalls, not scores.** Somebody who has not opened a lesson in a
  month needs a conversation. Somebody who scored L0 needs encouragement — that
  is where most people start.
- **Do the path yourself.** Nothing makes a team take it seriously faster.

## Team questions

**Somebody has not started.** Their card shows it. A conversation works better
than a reminder email.

**Somebody's path looks wrong for their job.** Their department or job title is
probably wrong in the system. Ask Learning & Development to check.

**Can I see the whole department?** Only if you have been given department
analytics. Otherwise: your direct reports.

**Can I add someone to my team?** No — reporting lines come from HR data. Ask
Learning & Development to correct it.
